MeetDone · Product research and design review

A meeting host's question: what still needs discussion, confirmation or a decision before we end?

The current product

Create a meeting → write goals → configure a structure and stage goals or required speakers → paste/import a transcript → analyze → review evidence and gaps → continue, assign follow-ups or record exceptions → end and download the summary.

The separate meeting-rules form has been removed. One goal document can contain topics, conclusions, decisions and action outputs. Explicit labeled lines are checked individually. Natural-language outcomes are assessed as part of goal coverage.

1. Research

The original research notes compared transcription, summaries, action items, agendas and integrations in Otter, Fireflies and Fellow, and explored GhostMeet and MeetingBro as open-source references. These were the author's impressions at the time, not a current capability audit.

The design hypothesis: recording what happened is useful, but a host also needs to know which required outcomes remain incomplete before ending. MeetDone concentrates on that question.

2. Product thinking

  • Keep the first interaction obvious; prefer a clear status and action over explanatory screens.
  • Templates describe reusable structure, not fixed participants or transcripts.
  • Four structures support different meeting styles: time, speakers, stages, and stage × speaker.
  • Speaking order guides discussion; required participation is what matters.

3. What is implemented

  • Chinese/English UI, local persistence, templates, editable goals and draggable structures.
  • Text and UTF-8 TXT input, up to 50,000 characters.
  • DeepSeek extraction of coverage, opinions, decisions, actions and line-linked evidence.
  • A deterministic engine classifies blockers and follow-ups. Incidental actions missing dates do not automatically block a meeting.
  • Recorded exceptions and an automatically downloaded Markdown summary.

4. Test entries and mock boundaries

Home provides fresh copies of three fictional meetings: a mobile-feature launch review with intentional gaps and an appendable follow-up, a sprint retrospective, and customer onboarding progress. Their transcript content is mock. Analysis is a real AI request and requires server configuration; failures never generate fake results.

Only a missing required outcome or an evidenced critical blocker prevents completion. Ordinary documentation and supporting-material gaps remain follow-ups.

5. Trade-offs and limitations

No accounts, database or cross-device sync. Data is stored in browser localStorage. No audio/video upload, live transcription, automatic live prompting or Teams/Feishu/Zoom/Google Meet integration. During meetings, users may update the transcript and analyze manually. Use explicit calendar dates; relative deadlines are not inferred. Review AI evidence because extraction can be wrong.

Future direction: speech-to-text and diarization feeding the existing transcript pipeline, followed by meeting-platform integrations. These are future work, not implemented capabilities.
